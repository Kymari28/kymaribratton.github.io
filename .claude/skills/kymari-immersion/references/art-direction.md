# Art Direction

## North star

KymariBratton.com is a professional UX portfolio that gradually reveals itself as an interactive digital world.

Desired visitor reactions, in order:

1. "Her work is strong."
2. "I want her to make something like this for us."
3. "That was beautiful."
4. "I've never seen a UX portfolio feel quite like that."

## Creative hierarchy

EDITORIAL
→ LIVING
→ DREAMLIKE

Not:

- cottagecore
- fairy garden
- cyberpunk
- generic tech portfolio
- animation demo reel

## Homepage

Protect the current hero.

The hero should clearly communicate:

"This is Kymari. This is what she does."

Do not replace it with an experimental landing screen.

Immersion increases as the visitor scrolls.

### Progression

Hero:
cream, pink, sage, editorial, clear.

Immediately after hero:
one tiny indication that the environment is alive.

Middle:
botanical art becomes more present.

Selected Work:
first major immersive payoff.

Case studies:
full project-specific world.

## Kymari's environment

Possible ingredients:

- cream
- blush pink
- sage
- lilies
- wildflowers
- stems
- leaves
- natural forms
- real botanical photography
- organic shadows
- subtle depth
- rare dragonfly

Botanical choices depend on composition.

Do not use the same flower everywhere.

The environment should feel like an ecosystem.

## Dragonfly

The dragonfly has personal meaning to Kymari.

It is NOT:

- a logo
- a mascot
- a cartoon guide
- required navigation

Use approximately 2 to 3 meaningful appearances across the main portfolio experience.

Possible behavior:

- atmospheric appearance
- subtle pointer response once
- rare directional placement

Finding it should feel special.

## Layering

Most environmental artwork remains behind content.

Rare foreground moments are allowed.

Good:

- stem behind a heading
- botanical edge entering a composition
- dragonfly passing through empty space
- sunlight falling across a surface

Bad:

- flowers covering paragraphs
- insects over important screenshots
- decoration over buttons
- constant foreground movement

## Visual reference philosophy

Immersive Garden may inspire:

- restraint
- sculptural environmental art
- negative space
- environmental depth
- quiet immersion
- material feeling
- compelling stillness
- desire to explore

Do not copy:

- layout
- branding
- artwork
- typography
- exact interactions
- composition

Kymari's own identity remains cream, pink, sage, warm, editorial, botanical, human, and expressive.
