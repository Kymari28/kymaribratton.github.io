How we got there

A team process, before my individual focus.

The wider redesign was a collaborative effort with the Develop for Good team, moving through:

  1. 1 Researching comparable nonprofit sites, including New York Cares and Robin Hood, for patterns worth adapting
  2. 2 Interviewing people across GRC's key audiences, one persona group per team member
  3. 3 Defining user personas and prioritizing the features and content each one actually needed
  4. 4 Moving from low-fidelity layouts to high-fidelity Figma designs built for Wix's constraints
  5. 5 Implementing in Wix, with custom code where its native components weren't enough
  6. 6 Testing with real personas on desktop and mobile, then refining from what we observed

From there, my own focus narrowed to three areas: Impact, Events, and Grant Writers.

Approach

One organization, three audiences.

Community organizations hold a lot of information that matters to very different readers. A resident wants to know what is available to them and how to access it. A partner organization wants to understand programs and reach. A funder wants evidence of impact. The work is about structuring one body of organizational information so it can answer all three without becoming a document dump.

Residents

People looking for programs and resources need to find what applies to them and understand how to access it, without needing to know how the organization is structured internally.

Partners & stakeholders

Partner organizations need a clear picture of what the council does, who it serves, and where collaboration fits.

Funders

Funder-facing readers need organizational data and impact information presented so it can actually be read and interpreted, not just published.

Process · Scale

The project was bigger than three pages.

Structuring one site to answer three very different audiences meant mapping everything the team heard onto an actual architecture — not just Impact, Events, and Grant Writers, but every program, resource, and pathway GRC needed a place for.

Process · Prioritization

Deciding what mattered most.

Interviews surfaced real friction: financial reports that stopped in 2020, no section highlighting GRC's impact, and event registration links that were inconsistent or broken. The team had a long list of things we could fix — not time to fix all of them at once.

“Why are events and registration different pages?”

Usability testing participant

Weighing impact against effort put the event calendar, accessibility, and a clearer impact story at the top — exactly the problems Impact, Events, and Grant Writers were built to solve.

My contribution · Grant Writers

Built for the reader who needs the numbers.

Grant funding is one of GRC's main sources of support, so I focused the Impact page on making that data easy for a grant writer to actually use: I coded the interactive element myself so someone can scan the key metrics first, then open "Explore Grant Highlights" for the year-by-year detail without sorting through a long list of statistics.

  • 10,200 Residents engaged
  • 5,680 Technical assistance hours
  • 196 Partnerships established

All six categories, with year-by-year detail, are explored just below.

What testing revealed. During testing, one participant expected the entire expandable area to be clickable, not just the small dropdown icon. It was a small detail, but it changed how I think about interaction targets: how "open for more detail" reads matters as much as whether the detail is there.

When the platform wasn't enough

Past what Wix's components could do.

Some of the interactions we wanted couldn't be built from Wix's standard components alone:

  • Calendar/Grid state
  • Search
  • Focus-area filtering
  • Date & event selection
  • Responsive behavior

I worked with custom JavaScript inside Wix's Velo environment to build that interaction logic myself, while keeping the page something GRC could still manage day to day.

Testing

Testing and client feedback

Across the team's usability testing, six-plus participants across desktop and mobile, I led the qualitative analysis of what came back: people found it easier to locate the information they needed and to understand what GRC is actually working on. Testing focused on the impact task specifically showed the clearest gain: average ease-of-use ratings rose from 2.5/5 to 4.5/5.

“Develop for Good's team transformed Gateway's website into a modern, accessible, and engaging platform that clearly communicates our mission and impact.”

Sherrie Cade, CEO & Founder, Gateway Regional Council
