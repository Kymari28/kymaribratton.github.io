The challenge

Calm, without making people figure it out themselves.

The challenge was never just "make a calming wellness website." Mindfulness with Desiree serves people arriving with very different needs: 1:1 coaching, The Regulated Woman group support, retreats, community experiences, corporate wellness, and the site needed to feel calm, warm, human, and unhurried without becoming unclear, dense, directionless, or generic. All of that inside a client timeline of about 2.5 weeks.

My focus · Emotional design + information hierarchy

Before designing

A wellness-site audit, not a blank page.

With a short timeline, I audited other wellness websites, including Prana Flo, to study service organization, emotional visual design, practitioner credibility, photography, and conversion without aggressive sales pressure. Three principles came out of it and carried into the decisions below.

  • Calm does not mean empty

    Information still needs hierarchy, even when the tone is unhurried.

  • Trust before conversion

    Visitors need to understand the practitioner and approach before being pushed toward a booking.

  • Different service, different path

    Corporate wellness can't use the same information hierarchy as someone seeking 1:1 support.

Discovery to booking.

My focus · Information architecture

Service organization

One brand, five reasons to arrive.

1:1 coaching, The Regulated Woman, retreats, community experiences, and corporate wellness each get their own space in the information architecture instead of competing in one list.

The Regulated Woman

A structured group program built around nervous-system regulation, with a clear booking flow, information hierarchy, and multi-week program structure.

1:1 coaching

Individual sessions built around a client's own nervous-system and stress needs.

Corporate wellness

Workshops for teams and organizations, the practice's most business-facing offering.

Retreats

In-person, multi-day experiences that bring nervous-system education, movement, and community together outside of a client's everyday environment.

Community experiences

Group mindfulness classes and sound-bath events designed for people who want a shared practice.

Calm before complexity

Emotional design required controlling information density, not only calming colors.

Calm still needs direction

Visitors still needed clear service pathways and calls to action.

Multiple users, one brand

The site needed to support individual, group, retreat/community, and corporate visitors.

Motion supports the feeling

The experience uses restrained reveals and pacing rather than constant movement.

In their words

“Understand your stress. Change your life.”

Mindfulness with Desiree

The site's real hook is nervous-system education, not generic wellness language. The information architecture had to carry that distinction: separate, legible paths for 1:1 coaching, the group program, and corporate wellness, so each visitor lands on the version of that message that's actually meant for them.

My focus · Audience strategy

Same brand, different decision-maker

A personal visitor and a corporate buyer need different things.

The brand, tone, and typography stay related across both. The image-to-text ratio is the difference: one visitor needs space and reassurance, the other needs structure and proof.

Personal wellness visitor

  • Connection and reassurance
  • Understanding Desiree and her approach
  • Service fit
  • A clear next step

Corporate wellness decision-maker

  • Credibility and organizational relevance
  • Program formats
  • Business value
  • Evidence: previous partnerships, like the Chipotle session shown earlier
  • A clear inquiry path

Approach

Routing each visitor to their own path.

Structuring the offering

The practice runs 1:1 coaching, a structured group program, corporate wellness workshops, and community events. A single undifferentiated site would have asked every visitor to work out for themselves which of those applied to them. The information architecture separates these routes so each audience can recognise itself quickly and reach the relevant next step.

Getting to a booking

Much of the practice's audience arrives from social media on a phone, so the path from landing on the site to booking a discovery call needed to be short and legible on a small screen. That shaped where calls to action sit, how many steps sit between interest and booking, and what someone needs to understand before they commit.

What's next

What I'd validate next.

This phase focused on strategy, structure, visual design, and implementation within a 2.5-week client timeline. Formal usability testing wasn't part of that phase, so the next step I'd prioritize is validating:

  • Can a first-time visitor understand what the practice offers quickly?
  • Can someone identify the pathway most relevant to them, 1:1, group, corporate, without confusion?
  • Are the calls to action clear at the moment someone is ready to act?
  • Does the corporate experience serve a decision-maker differently from the individual experience?
  • Can people move between offerings without feeling overwhelmed?
