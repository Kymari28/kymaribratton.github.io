Mindfulness with Desiree

Designing calm, without losing clarity.

Website strategy and information architecture for a wellness practice, focused on how people move from first discovering the practice to actually booking.

The information architecture gives individual, group-program, and corporate audiences clearer routes to the right offering and booking path.

Project details

  • Organization. Mindfulness with Desiree, a wellness practice.
  • Sector. Wellness, coaching, and corporate wellness.
  • Focus. Website strategy, information architecture, and digital experience.
  • Context. Wellness-focused internship project.
  • Timeline. Completed for the client in about 2.5 weeks.
  • Status. Live.

My role

What I focused on

I focused on website strategy, information architecture, and the multiple audience journeys running through one brand: call-to-action strategy, service organization, mobile pathways, digital resources, social-to-website conversion, and corporate wellness positioning, translating Desiree's wellness identity into a clearer digital experience.

A 2.5-week timeline meant prioritizing: I focused on clear information architecture, support for multiple audience pathways, a consistent emotional visual system, and useful calls to action, rather than trying to perfect every page equally, a prioritization constraint, not an excuse.

The challenge

Calm, without making people figure it out themselves.

The challenge was never just "make a calming wellness website." Mindfulness with Desiree serves people arriving with very different needs, 1:1 coaching, The Regulated Woman group support, retreats, community experiences, corporate wellness, and I wanted the site to feel calm, warm, human, and unhurried without becoming unclear, dense, directionless, or generic. All of that inside a client timeline of about 2.5 weeks.

My focus · Emotional design + information hierarchy

Before designing

A wellness-site audit, not a blank page.

With a short timeline, I audited other wellness websites, including Prana Flo, to study service organization, emotional visual design, practitioner credibility, photography, and conversion without aggressive sales pressure. Three principles came out of it and carried into the decisions below.

  • Calm does not mean empty

    Information still needs hierarchy, even when the tone is unhurried.

  • Trust before conversion

    Visitors need to understand the practitioner and approach before being pushed toward a booking.

  • Different service, different path

    Corporate wellness can't use the same information hierarchy as someone seeking 1:1 support.

My focus · Audience strategy

Same brand, different decision-maker

A personal visitor and a corporate buyer need different things.

The brand, tone, and typography stay related across both. The image-to-text ratio is the difference: one visitor needs space and reassurance, the other needs structure and proof.

Personal wellness visitor

  • Connection and reassurance
  • Understanding Desiree and her approach
  • Service fit
  • A clear next step

Corporate wellness decision-maker

  • Credibility and organizational relevance
  • Program formats
  • Business value

My focus · CTA + booking pathways

Approach

A calm experience still needs a clear next action.

Discovery, understanding the practice, choosing a service, and taking a next step: not one linear funnel, but every one of those four stages still needed a clear resolution, whatever path a visitor was on.

Structuring the offering

The practice runs 1:1 coaching, a structured group program, corporate wellness workshops, and community events. The information architecture separates these routes so each audience can recognize itself quickly and reach the relevant next step, rather than working it out from one undifferentiated page.

Getting to a booking

Much of the practice's audience arrives from social media on a phone, so the path from landing on the site to booking needed to be short and legible on a small screen. That shaped where calls to action sit and how many steps sit between interest and booking.

What's next

What I'd validate next.

This phase focused on strategy, structure, visual design, and implementation within a 2.5-week client timeline. Formal usability testing wasn't part of that phase, so the next step I'd prioritize is validating:

  • Can a first-time visitor understand what the practice offers quickly?
  • Can someone identify the pathway most relevant to them, 1:1, group, corporate, without confusion?
  • Are the calls to action clear at the moment someone is ready to act?
  • Does the corporate experience serve a decision-maker differently from the individual experience?
  • Can people move between offerings without feeling overwhelmed?

Reflection

Clarity doesn't have to feel clinical.

Emotional design is not separate from usability. Calm still requires hierarchy. Beautiful experiences still need direction. Information density affects emotional experience, and pacing affects clarity. Different audiences may need different structures even within one brand, and visual feeling and usability can support each other rather than compete.

The live site is the clearest view of the outcome. I'm happy to walk through the information architecture, the audience pathways, or the design decisions in more detail.
